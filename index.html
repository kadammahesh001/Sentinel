Full Syllabus

DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) is a distilled, compact language model that retains 97% of BERT-base's language understanding with 40% fewer parameters and 60% faster inference. Knowledge Distillation trains a compact "student" network (6 layers) under the supervision of a larger "teacher" network (12 layers) using a triple loss function: soft cross-entropy on temperature-softened probabilities (\(\mathcal{L}_{ce}\)), masked language modeling loss (\(\mathcal{L}_{mlm}\)), and cosine hidden state distance (\(\mathcal{L}_{cos}\)).

Scaled Dot-Product Attention is formulated as: $$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$ The query (\(Q\)) and key (\(K\)) projections determine pairwise token compatibility scores. Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) prevents vanishing gradients in the softmax. In multi-head attention (12 heads), the model simultaneously attends to syntactic, semantic, and contextual relationships across multiple subspaces.

We implemented a zero-leakage protocol:
  1. The 1,000 held-out test samples were carved exclusively from the official stanfordnlp/imdb test split.
  2. The 2,000 training and 500 validation samples were sampled exclusively from the official training split.
  3. Random seed 42 was pinned across Python, NumPy, PyTorch, and CUDA/MPS for exact split reproducibility.
  4. Zero test texts or tokens were ever seen by the optimizer during model training or checkpoint selection.

An untuned pretrained DistilBERT checkpoint has its classification head weights randomly initialized. Without domain fine-tuning, its predictions hover around random chance (or lower due to arbitrary head initialization bias, producing 40.00% accuracy on our held-out test split). Supervised fine-tuning with Cross-Entropy Loss adapts all 6 encoder blocks and calibrates the classification head, achieving 84.10% accuracy (+44.10% lift) and an F1-score of 0.8349.

Standard Adam applies L2 regularization to gradients, which distorts weight decay when combined with momentum. AdamW decouples weight decay (\(0.01\)) directly from gradient updates, stabilizing transformer optimization. The linear warmup (25 steps) prevents early large parameter updates from destroying rich pretrained transformer features, after which linear decay smoothly anneals the learning rate towards zero.

WordPiece decomposes unknown words into frequent sub-word tokens with ## prefixes (e.g., "unforgettable" → ["un", "##forget", "##table"]). This guarantees that any arbitrary string can be represented without generating [UNK] tokens while maintaining a bounded vocabulary size (30,522 tokens).

No. Softmax confidence reflects mathematical exponentiation of logit scores: \(\sigma(z_i) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}\). It measures the statistical alignment of token sequences with IMDb training patterns. Sarcasm, double negation, or out-of-domain text can yield high confidence despite incorrect or misleading predictions. Hence, calibration disclosures are essential in ethical AI engineering.

  • DistilBERT (Encoder-only): Uses bidirectional attention where each token sees past and future context simultaneously. Ideal for sequence classification, regression, and token labeling. Parameters: ~66M. Latency: < 25ms.
  • GPT / Llama (Decoder-only): Uses causal masked attention where tokens can only attend to previous tokens. Ideal for autoregressive open-ended text generation. Parameters: 7B to 70B+. Requires significantly greater memory and compute.

Probability Calibration vs. Truth

Ethical AI
Softmax Normalization:
$$P(y = 1 | x) = \frac{e^{z_1}}{e^{z_0} + e^{z_1}}$$

Cross-entropy training optimizes log-likelihood over the labeled training distribution. However, neural networks can be overconfident on out-of-distribution or adversarial inputs.

  • Sarcasm vulnerability: "Oh great, another masterpiece of cinematic garbage" yields conflicting tokens.
  • Confidence scores indicate classification probability, not factual truth.
  • Safety bounds: In production systems, predictions with confidence below 70% are flagged for human-in-the-loop review.
